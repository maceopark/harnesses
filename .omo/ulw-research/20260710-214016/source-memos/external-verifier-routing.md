# External evidence/tool verifiers versus intrinsic LLM self-correction

Scope: empirical evidence for routing `ultimateinterview` claims to intrinsic critique, external tools, learned verifiers, executable checkers, or human authority. This is not a claim that one verifier class works universally. All reported results retain their paper's task, model, and date boundary.

## Primary-paper evidence

| Study | Scope actually tested | Result relevant to routing | Boundary / negative result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Huang et al., [Large Language Models Cannot Self-Correct Reasoning Yet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798), first posted 2023-10-03; ICLR 2024 | GPT-3.5-Turbo (`0613`) and GPT-4 accessed 2023-08-29; GSM8K, CommonSenseQA, HotpotQA; at most two correction rounds. Additional intrinsic runs used GPT-4-Turbo and Llama-2-70B. | Oracle correctness labels improved GPT-3.5 from 75.9 to 84.3 on GSM8K, 75.8 to 89.7 on CSQA, and 26.0 to 29.0 on HotpotQA; GPT-4 improved 95.5 to 97.5, 82.0 to 85.5, and 49.0 to 59.0. | Removing oracle labels made intrinsic correction neutral or harmful: GPT-3.5 after two rounds was 74.7 / 41.8 / 25.0; GPT-4 was 89.0 / 80.0 / 43.0. This falsifies treating a same-model review loop as a generic correctness gate for those reasoning tasks, not for all models/tasks. |
| Tyen et al., [LLMs cannot find reasoning errors, but can correct them given the error location](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.08516), first posted 2023-11-14 | GPT-4/4-Turbo, GPT-3.5-Turbo, Gemini Pro, PaLM 2 Unicorn; five BIG-Bench-style logical tasks with annotated first-error locations. | Exact error-location feedback let PaLM 2 backtracking repair originally wrong traces by +18.04 to +43.92 percentage points depending on task. It isolates localization as a useful external signal. | GPT-4's best overall mistake-location accuracy was only 52.87%. Even oracle localization changed some originally correct final answers to wrong ones (-0 to -11.43 points), and at an assumed 99% base accuracy one task's correction policy would reduce accuracy to 92.8%. The authors' small out-of-domain classifier did not meet their effective-correction threshold. |
| Gou et al., [CRITIC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11738), first posted 2023-05-19; ICLR 2024 | Text-Davinci-003, GPT-3.5-Turbo, Llama-2 7B/13B/70B; sampled free-form QA, mathematical program synthesis, and toxicity reduction; 2023-era Google search, Python execution, Perspective API. | Tool-interactive correction beat no-tool correction in the reported tasks; e.g. ChatGPT HotpotQA F1 rose from CoT 42.8 to CRITIC 52.9, while CRITIC without tools was 46.1. On 100 analyzed HotpotQA cases, hallucination errors fell from 36% of CoT errors to 7% of CRITIC errors. | These tools were not sound or complete. On GSM8K, CRITIC repaired 32.2% of initially erroneous programs but reduced accuracy on originally correct outputs by 4.3%; wrong corrections were 14.3% of the analyzed post-correction error pool. In the HotpotQA error sample, 10% were incorrect corrections and 12% refusals. Search freshness, prompt demonstrations, textual modality, task/model transfer, tool bias, and latency were explicit limitations. |
| Stechly et al., [On the Self-Verification Limitations of Large Language Models on Reasoning and Planning Tasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08115), first posted 2024-02-12; ICLR 2025 | GPT-4; 100 instances each of Game of 24, graph coloring, Blocksworld, and obfuscated Mystery Blocksworld; task-specific SymPy/graph/VAL-style checkers. | Sound checking plus repeated proposals improved accuracy over standard prompting: 5 to 36-38 (Game of 24), 16 to 34-38 (coloring), 40 to 60-87 (Blocksworld), and 4 to 6-10 (Mystery Blocksworld). A checker that never rejects a valid solution preserves already-correct accepted outputs within the modeled predicate. | The GPT-4-as-verifier loop scored 3, 2, 55, and 0 respectively: worse than standard prompting in three tasks and a modest gain in Blocksworld, still below sound verification. Its false-negative rates reached 95.8% on coloring and 97.09% on Mystery Blocksworld. Binary feedback, first-error feedback, and all-error feedback were similar; repeated unchanged prompting plus the sound checker retained most gains. Thus the observed benefit was largely search plus trustworthy acceptance, not rich critique comprehension. Results cover small formal domains, not open-world requirements. |
| Cobbe et al., [Training Verifiers to Solve Math Word Problems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14168), first posted 2021-10-27 | Separate learned generators/verifiers up to 175B; GSM8K; verifier ranks 100 generated solutions. | With enough in-domain data, verifier-ranked search outperformed finetuning; a 6B verifier system slightly exceeded a finetuned 175B generator in the reported setup. Separate generation and selection is therefore empirically useful when local labeled validation exists. | Labels used final-answer equality, so flawed reasoning with a correct result became false positives. Small training sets did not help, solution-level verifiers overfit, and generator diversity mattered. A learned verifier supplies empirical discrimination, not proof of correctness. |
| Lightman et al., [Let's Verify Step by Step](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.20050), first posted 2023-05-31 | MATH, a large reward model, best-of-N search, PRM800K human step labels; a held-out 234-question set of recent AP/AMC tests. | Process reward models beat outcome reward models and majority vote across tested N; the best reported process-supervised system solved 78.2% of the representative MATH subset. Step localization and human process labels can make a learned verifier more useful than final-answer-only supervision. | The reward system remains only as reliable as its labels/model. The strongest small-model ablations used the large PRM as a labeling oracle; iterative selector retraining was unstable and produced no improvement. OOD evidence was modest and math-specific, not a general transition guarantee. |
| Madaan et al., [Self-Refine](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17651), first posted 2023-03-30; NeurIPS 2023 | GPT-3.5, ChatGPT, GPT-4, and Codex; seven tasks spanning preference-heavy text, constrained generation, code optimization/readability, and GSM8K math. | Same-model feedback/refinement improved the paper's aggregate, especially preference/constrained-generation and code-quality tasks. It is useful as a proposal/refinement mechanism where multiple outputs are acceptable. | Math gains were effectively zero: GPT-3.5 64.1 to 64.1, ChatGPT 74.8 to 75.0, GPT-4 92.9 to 93.1. The authors attributed this to failure to identify errors and reported 5%+ gains only when an external source identified incorrect math answers. In their failure analysis, 33% arose from wrong error localization and 61% from an inappropriate suggested fix. Positive aggregate refinement results must not be relabeled as factual verification. |

## Verifier-routing matrix for `ultimateinterview`

| Claim / transition | Primary verifier route | Role allowed for intrinsic LLM critique | Transition rule | Failure state |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Exact syntax, schema, digest, path, command availability, deterministic arithmetic | Executable parser/linter/hash/checker against the current artifact and host | Generate candidate fix or additional negative case | A checker may authorize its *exact predicate* only. Record tool, version/environment, observation time, predicate, and output in a current `from-code`/`from-scenario` evidence record. It does not settle the enclosing behavioral requirement. | Checker unavailable, stale, malformed, or predicate mismatch -> `Blocked`; checker disagreement -> `Contested`. |
| Runtime behavior with an executable oracle (exit code, API contract, persisted state, invariant) | Real-surface test or domain checker; preserve an independently observed pre-state | Propose trials; interpret failures provisionally | A passing observation can establish only the exercised operation x state x boundary path. Require negative cases and the real surface already demanded by `Verification commands`; never infer untested-path completeness. | Fail or non-reproducible -> score stays at least 2; inconsistent runs -> `Contested`; unsafe test unavailable -> structured `Deferred`. |
| Repository fact, API behavior, configuration, or documentation claim without a complete executable oracle | Direct current source/docs/runtime record, preferably two causal groups for critical claims | Locate candidate spans and counterexamples, never self-certify the summary | Fold external observations into typed evidence. The model's paraphrase is derived from the source and keeps its `independence_group`; it adds no second group. A search/retrieval result challenges or supports but cannot alone authorize score 0 if freshness, applicability, or source authority is unresolved. | Missing provenance/freshness -> hypothesis-only; conflict -> `Collide evidence`, `Contested`, user decides which authority governs only when that is a legitimate product decision. |
| Stakeholder intent, policy preference, scope, non-goal, risk acceptance | Named owner or delegated decision authority | Pressure-test wording, generate scenarios and scope-loss counterexamples | No tool can prove a normative choice. Use `Accepted` only for an explicit current owner/delegate decision; this is the existing single-source authority override and must not be counted as epistemic triangulation. | Unknown/unauthorized speaker -> `Blocked`; owner conflict -> `Contested`; defer only with owner/date. |
| High-impact user claim that also asserts an empirical fact | Split the claim: owner decides the desired policy; external evidence checks the factual premise | Ask pressure follow-up and propose falsifier | `pressure: survived` can confirm intent but cannot turn a factual assertion into verified evidence. Keep the empirical child entry at score >=2 until routed externally or explicitly recorded as an accepted assumption/risk. | Unsplit mixed claim -> abstain from settlement. |
| Open-world completeness, unknown unknowns, adequacy of threat/lens coverage | No complete verifier exists; use fresh open-world sweep, contrarian probe, checkpoint, and independent fresh-context review as bounded searches | Generate missing candidates and adversarial cases | Preserve existing rule that `no-material-divergence` / `inconclusive` gives zero evidence or completeness credit. Two dry sweeps satisfy a procedural stopping condition only, not proof that no gap exists. | Any material divergence reopens the gap, freshness, checkpoint, and Build Contract review. |
| Subjective quality, readability, wording, option generation | Human preference or explicit rubric with named authority | Self-Refine is allowed to generate/edit candidates | Intrinsic refinement may improve a candidate, but cannot add an eligible causal group or lower ambiguity about factual behavior. Human/rubric acceptance owns the transition. | No authority/rubric -> retain as preference assumption, never `Triangulated`. |
| Learned verifier / LLM critic verdict | Locally validated critic with held-out sensitivity analysis, or an independent reviewer used as advisory evidence | May critique, localize suspected error, or rank candidates | Never treat model identity alone as independence. Record training/eval scope, base rate, false-accept/false-reject costs, and current calibration. Unless a project policy defines and validates an acceptable false-accept bound, the verdict may reject/challenge or prioritize review but may not alone authorize score 0 / `implementation_ready`. | OOD, uncalibrated, or correlated with generator -> advisory/hypothesis-only; disagreement -> `Contested`. |
| Final Build Contract readiness | Deterministic `session_status.py --gate` plus fresh-implementer test and real-surface verification design | Fresh critic searches for missing questions and gameable criteria | Keep `--gate` as the sole emitter of `implementation_ready: yes`, but narrow its guarantee: structural/protocol conformance and declared predicate coverage. Fresh-implementer review remains a falsification search, not certification. | Any stale digest, missing command, gameable criterion, new gap, or unresolved contest blocks readiness. |

## Fail-closed abstention policy

1. **Classify before verifying.** Mark the claim as machine-decidable, runtime-observable, documentary/factual, normative, subjective, or open-world. A verifier may not authorize a transition outside its class.
2. **Bind the acceptance predicate.** Write what exact proposition a `pass` establishes, on which artifact/environment and at what time. If this cannot be stated, the verdict is advisory.
3. **Prefer acceptance soundness over correction fluency.** A task-specific checker may accept within its predicate. A web tool, learned verifier, or LLM critic may surface counterevidence and candidate corrections, but a `looks good` verdict is not settlement.
4. **Do not overwrite the last verified state on a fallible rejection.** Fork a candidate correction, run the verifier again, and commit the replacement only after the same acceptance route passes. This contains the wrong-correction mode observed by CRITIC and mistake-localization studies.
5. **Separate false accepts from false rejects.** False accepts threaten safety/readiness; false rejects consume time and may block liveness. No universal accuracy threshold is defensible from these papers. Set a local bound from consequence, base rate, and held-out validation; absent that policy, abstain.
6. **Treat same-lineage outputs as correlated.** Generator, self-critic, paraphrase of a tool result, and later refinement remain one causal group unless a genuinely independent observation establishes the claim. More tokens, agents, votes, or rounds do not manufacture independence.
7. **On disagreement, stop the transition.** Factual conflicts become `Contested`; normative conflicts go to the named authority; unavailable necessary evidence becomes `Blocked` or a structured `Deferred` risk. Never average incompatible verdicts into confidence.
8. **Preserve liveness explicitly.** A sound but incomplete checker can reject forever or time out. Bound attempts/cost; on exhaustion, retain the last verified artifact and surface the unresolved gap rather than accepting an unchecked candidate.

## Preserved and lost guarantees

| Route | Guarantee preserved | Guarantee not obtained / lost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sound deterministic checker + immutable/current input | If implemented correctly, acceptance implies the encoded predicate; already accepted valid candidates are not destroyed by continued search; result is replayable with environment/provenance. | Specification adequacy, checker implementation correctness, unencoded requirements, real-world freshness outside the observation, and open-world completeness. Soundness costs liveness when the generator never proposes a valid candidate. |
| Real-surface test | Evidence that the observed artifact exhibited the asserted outcome under the named state and boundary traversal. | Other states, concurrency/timing regimes, future versions, hidden side effects, and causal explanation. |
| External fallible tool (search, execution error, toxicity API) | An independent observation channel when provenance and causal lineage are genuine; often better error localization than intrinsic critique. | No general soundness/completeness; tool bias, stale/index-missing evidence, metric gaming, and wrong corrections remain. |
| Learned process verifier | Empirically improved ranking/localization on validated distributions; finer credit assignment than final-answer-only labels in the tested math setting. | Proof of correctness, robustness to OOD/adversarial outputs, independence from training/generator errors, and immunity to reward hacking. |
| Learned outcome verifier | Empirical candidate ranking when sufficiently trained and locally validated. | Correct reasoning: a right answer reached through a flawed process may be a false positive. |
| Intrinsic critique / self-refinement | Cheap generation of alternatives, stylistic improvement, rubric decomposition, possible discovery of candidate flaws. | Correctness, independent corroboration, monotonic improvement, reliable stopping, and an eligible second causal group. |
| Owner decision | Authority for desired policy/scope and explicit risk acceptance. | Empirical truth, implementation feasibility, or independent corroboration. |
| Dry sweep / contrarian / fresh-context review | Evidence that bounded, named search procedures were executed and what they observed. | Completeness or proof of absence. |

## Falsifiers for the routing recommendation

Revise the policy if any of the following is demonstrated on the actual `ultimateinterview` workload, not merely on a benchmark with a different verifier contract:

1. A preregistered, contamination-controlled evaluation shows same-model intrinsic critique monotonically reduces both false accepts and false rejects across representative brownfield claim classes, including already-correct inputs, without oracle stop labels or weaker initial prompts.
2. An LLM critic independently meets a predeclared, consequence-derived false-accept bound on held-out and adversarially generated local cases, retains calibration under the generator/model/version changes used in production, and outperforms the external route at equal cost.
3. A fallible search/tool feedback pipeline demonstrates no destructive regressions on an adequately powered suite of initially correct cases and supplies stable provenance/freshness sufficient to replay every accepted transition.
4. The deterministic gate is mutation-tested: plausible specification omissions and checker mutations cannot still yield `implementation_ready: yes`. Passing this would expand what the gate can claim; failing it confirms the present narrow structural guarantee.
5. Causal-lineage experiments show that multiple same-model roles produce error independence comparable to genuinely distinct observations after controlling prompts, samples, and shared training data. Until then, count lineage, not agent labels.
6. A formal or empirical completeness argument is produced for the actual open-world sweep/probe space. Without it, dry runs remain procedural convergence only.

## OBSERVATIONS

- The strongest repeated empirical pattern is not “feedback works,” but “reliable error detection/acceptance is the bottleneck.” Huang and Tyen isolate failure to know *when/where* an answer is wrong; Stechly shows that sound acceptance plus repeated generation can retain most gains even when rich critique is removed.
- External is not synonymous with trustworthy. CRITIC's search/execution/API tools improved task metrics yet still damaged correct answers. Tool provenance, freshness, applicability, and false-accept behavior determine whether the result is evidence or only a lead.
- Positive intrinsic-refinement evidence is task-shaped. Self-Refine improved subjective/constrained/code-quality outputs but produced near-zero math gains; its aggregate should support candidate refinement, not correctness settlement.
- Learned verifiers occupy a middle tier: useful selectors/localizers under validated distributions, but neither sound checkers nor independent truth sources by default.

## CLAIMS

- `ultimateinterview` should route verification by claim type and verifier contract, not by a blanket “critic” role.
- Only a sound, current, scoped checker should be allowed to authorize the exact machine-decidable predicate it checks. Even then, it does not prove specification adequacy or open-world completeness.
- Intrinsic critique should be restricted to proposal generation, adversarial search, and subjective refinement. It should never create an eligible independence group or directly lower a factual gap to settled.
- Fallible external tools and learned critics should be asymmetric: strong enough to challenge, reject provisionally, localize, and prioritize; insufficient alone to accept high-impact factual claims unless locally validated against an explicit false-accept policy.
- The existing `Contested` / `Blocked` / structured `Deferred`, causal `independence_group`, owner-authority override, stale-review invalidation, real-surface verification, and sole `--gate` readiness transition are the right control points; the key addition is an explicit verifier-class and acceptance-predicate binding.

## EXPAND

- Specify an evidence-record extension such as `verifier_class`, `predicate`, `artifact_digest`, `tool_version`, `false_accept_policy`, and `coverage_scope`; determine which fields can be enforced without bloating routine interviews.
- Mutation-test `session_status.py --gate`, `verification_lint.py`, and representative Build Contracts to measure specification/checker adequacy rather than assuming it.
- Build a local verifier benchmark from historical `ultimateinterview` postmortems: already-correct claims, factual conflicts, stale docs, gameable acceptance criteria, subjective requirements, and owner-only decisions. Measure false accepts separately from false rejects.
- Test whether the cross-vendor `critic` binding reduces correlated failures in practice. Do not grant independence merely because the vendor differs; use blind, source-minimized tasks and compare error overlap.
- Define bounded retry/timeout policies for sound-but-incomplete checkers so fail-closed safety does not become silent nontermination.
