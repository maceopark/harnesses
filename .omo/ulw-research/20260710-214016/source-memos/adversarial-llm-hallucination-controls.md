# Empirical LLM Reliability Controls for ultimateinterview

Research completed 2026-07-10 (America/Los_Angeles). This memo separates what was observed in each paper's evaluated setting from design implications. It does not treat benchmark gains as universal guarantees.

## Method and coverage

- Ran 20 distinct academic/official web queries across retrieval/RAG, tool use, self-consistency, intrinsic and tool-grounded critique, debate, scalable oversight, verifier models, process supervision, uncertainty, abstention, calibration, and correlated errors. Queries deliberately included negative-result, failure-mode, replication, robustness, distractor, and reward-hacking terms.
- Fetched and converted 15 full papers for close reading: Lewis et al. 2020; Cobbe et al. 2021; Wang et al. 2022; Kadavath et al. 2022; Yao et al. 2022; Gou et al. 2023; Du et al. 2023; Lightman et al. 2023; Huang et al. 2024; Chen et al. 2024; Kenton et al. 2024; Abbasi-Yadkori et al. 2024; Kim et al. 2025; Amiraz et al. 2025; Stechly et al. 2025. Also read the full open-access Nature article by Farquhar et al. 2024 and primary abstracts/results for Uesato et al. 2022 and Tian et al. 2023.
- Evidence below records paper date, evaluated model(s), tasks, and the boundary beyond which the result should not be generalized.

## Evidence matrix

| Method | Primary evidence, model/task/date scope | Observed result | Failure boundary / counter-evidence |

| Retrieval grounding | [Lewis et al., 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401): RAG built on BART + DPR/Wikipedia; NQ, TriviaQA, WebQuestions, CuratedTREC, MS-MARCO, Jeopardy question generation, FEVER. | RAG beat parametric BART on knowledge-intensive tasks. Non-informative search caused 23% of sampled ReAct failure cases and repetitive reasoning caused errors. Tool access changes the failure surface; it does not remove failure. |
| Retrieval robustness | [Amiraz et al., ACL 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.892/): Llama 3.1/3.2/3.3, Qwen and Gemma variants; NQ, PopQA, TriviaQA, WebQuestions. | Adding a hard irrelevant passage alongside a gold passage reduced NQ accuracy by 6-11 points depending on model, including Llama-3.3-70B. Distractor rankings were highly correlated across tested models. | Shared retrieval is a common-mode dependency: source relevance, source truth, and claim support must be checked separately. A citation-shaped passage can make multiple models agree on the same error. |
| Tool-grounded critique | [Gou et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11738): ChatGPT/gpt-3.5-turbo, text-davinci-003 and open models; AmbigNQ, TriviaQA, HotpotQA, program synthesis and toxicity tasks. | CRITIC's search, interpreter and toxicity-tool feedback improved iterative revisions; the paper reports +7.7 F1 averaged over three QA datasets for ChatGPT and emphasizes external feedback. | The authors' ablations found critique without tools gave modest gains or degraded performance. Search feedback itself can be inaccurate/outdated; interpreter feedback is stronger only where the property is executable. |
| Self-consistency | [Wang et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11171): UL2-20B, GPT-3-175B, LaMDA-137B, PaLM-540B; arithmetic and commonsense benchmarks. | Sampling diverse CoT paths and majority voting improved GSM8K +17.9, SVAMP +11.0, AQuA +12.2, StrategyQA +6.4 and ARC-Challenge +3.9 points in reported best settings. | These are mostly single-answer math/commonsense tasks on 2022 models. Agreement measures modal model belief, not truth, and assumes useful diversity. |
| Self-consistency negative result | [Chen et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02419): GPT-3.5-turbo-0125; MMLU Physics, TruthfulQA, GPQA, AVeriTeC; 1,000-run averages. | Vote and Filter-Vote accuracy could increase and then decrease as calls scaled. More calls improved easy queries but reinforced wrong answers on hard queries. | The study is restricted to objective tasks with small answer spaces; it does not directly evaluate open-ended interview artifacts. It nevertheless refutes a monotonic "more agreement is safer" rule. |
| Intrinsic self-critique | [Huang et al., ICLR 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798): GPT-3.5-turbo-0613 full sets; GPT-4, GPT-4-Turbo and Llama-2-70B mostly 200 samples; GSM8K, CommonSenseQA, 100 HotpotQA; max two correction rounds. | Oracle-stopped correction improved scores, but intrinsic correction reduced accuracy across all tested benchmarks. GPT-3.5 GSM8K fell 75.9 to 74.7 after two rounds; GPT-4 fell 95.5 to 89.0. | The result concerns reasoning in these model/task/prompt settings, not stylistic or safety revision. It shows that reflection prose is not evidence and oracle leakage can masquerade as self-correction. |
| Sound versus learned verification | [Stechly et al., ICLR 2025](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/f3c5e56274140e0420baa3916c529210-Abstract-Conference.html): GPT-4-0613; 100 instances each of Game of 24, graph coloring, Blocksworld and obfuscated Blocksworld; up to 15 feedback rounds. | Same-LLM verification reduced accuracy from 5% to 3%, 16% to 2%, and 4% to 0% on three of four domains. Sound SymPy/constraint/VAL verifiers raised them to 36-38%, 34-38%, and 6-10%; repeated sampling plus a sound accept/reject check retained most gains without critique content. | Soundness is scoped to the formal predicate encoded by the checker. The result does not make informal requirements decidable, but it sharply distinguishes executable verification from persuasive critique. |
| Debate | [Du et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14325): copies of gpt-3.5-turbo-0301; arithmetic (100), GSM8K, chess (300), chess validity (100), 524 biographies, sampled MMLU. | Multi-agent debate improved several reasoning/factuality metrics over their baselines; some initially all-wrong groups later reached a correct answer. | Same-model agents were "agreeable"; longer contexts emphasized recent turns; debates sometimes converged on incorrect answers that all agents confidently affirmed. Factual biography evaluation itself used ChatGPT, coupling generator and evaluator. |
| Scalable-oversight debate counter-study | [Kenton et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04622): Gemma-7B, GPT-3.5, Gemini Pro 1.0/1.5 judges; Gemini Pro 1.5 debaters; 9 extractive, closed and multimodal binary QA tasks, 128 questions each, about 5M calls. | Debate beat consultancy, especially when a wrong consultant could mislead the judge. Against direct QA, debate helped under extractive information asymmetry but had small/no advantage on closed tasks; setup changes had little effect. | Authors call the evidence weakly promising and inference-only. Gains were narrower than prior QuALITY results; weak judges suffered long-context formatting failures and possible same-family preference bias. This is not evidence that free-form same-model debate validates requirements. |
| Outcome verifier | [Cobbe et al., 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14168): GPT-3-family 6B/175B generators/verifiers; GSM8K 8.5K. | Best-of-N verifier selection significantly improved GSM8K and scaled better with data than generator fine-tuning; estimated benefit was comparable to a 30x model-size increase. | Labels checked only final answers, so flawed reasoning could be positive. The paper says verifiers may use coarse heuristics; beyond 400 candidates, 6B performance declined as search found adversarial solutions that fooled the verifier. |
| Process supervision | [Lightman et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.20050): GPT-4-derived base/reward models; representative MATH subset; PRM800K with 800K human step labels. | A process reward model beat outcome supervision in best-of-N selection and solved 78.2% of the representative MATH subset; active learning improved label efficiency. | Large-scale ORM and PRM datasets were not directly comparable; the paper explicitly evaluates reward-model selection on math, not proof of general truthful reasoning. Human step labels are expensive and can encode annotator/model blind spots. |
| Process-vs-outcome counter-result | [Uesato et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14275): GSM8K math word problems. | Pure outcome supervision reached similar final-answer error with less label supervision; process feedback mainly improved reasoning correctness, reducing reasoning error among final-answer-correct solutions from 14.0% to 3.4%. | Process traces are useful for diagnosis and preventing lucky answers, but do not make executable outcome checks redundant. The evidence is a single math domain. |
| Self-evaluated confidence | [Kadavath et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05221): 800M, 3B, 12B, 52B pretrained LMs; BIG-Bench MC/TF plus open QA, math and code tasks. | Larger models could predict P(True) for proposed answers; P(IK) had useful discrimination and responded to sources/hints. | Calibration depended strongly on format. P(IK), trained on TriviaQA, generalized only partially and was poorly calibrated on new task distributions. A raw confidence number is not portable across domains/models/prompts. |
| Verbalized calibration | [Tian et al., EMNLP 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.330/): ChatGPT, GPT-4, Claude and open RLHF LMs; TriviaQA, SciQ, TruthfulQA. | Verbalized probabilities were typically better calibrated than conditional token probabilities, often reducing ECE by about 50% relatively. | Relative ECE improvement is not correctness and is benchmark/prompt/model dependent. Counter-evidence from [Groot & Valdenegro-Toro, 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.trustnlp-1.13/) found GPT-4, GPT-3.5, Llama-2 and PaLM-2 verbal uncertainty highly miscalibrated/overconfident on their LLM/VLM tasks. |
| Semantic uncertainty | [Farquhar et al., Nature 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07421-0): Llama-2, Falcon, Mistral plus GPT-4 paragraph experiments; 400 train/400 test samples each from BioASQ, SQuAD, TriviaQA, SVAMP, NQ-Open. | Meaning-cluster entropy detected random-seed-sensitive confabulations across free-form QA/math and supported selective refusal. | Authors explicitly say it does not detect systematic falsehoods, systematic reasoning errors or reward-driven deception. Low entropy can mean confidently wrong. It also requires repeated samples and semantic equivalence judgments. |
| Calibrated abstention | [Abbasi-Yadkori et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.01563): Gemini Pro; TriviaQA subset and full BIG-Bench Temporal Sequences. | Conformal calibration over response-similarity scores controlled empirical hallucination/error risk more reliably than uncalibrated thresholds, with less conservative abstention on long answers than log probability. | Guarantees require a labeled, exchangeable calibration set and a valid loss/similarity function. They do not transfer automatically under model, prompt, task or data drift. The evaluation covered two QA datasets and one model family. |
| Correlated model errors | [Kim et al., ICML 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07962): 349 models x 12,032 HuggingFace leaderboard questions; 71 x 14,042 HELM questions; 20 x 1,800 resume-job pairs. | On HELM, model pairs selected the same wrong answer about 60% of the time conditional on both being wrong. Shared provider/architecture increased correlation; more individually accurate models also had more correlated errors, even across providers. | Multiple-choice and numeric resume scoring are not open-ended requirement interviews. The study still invalidates treating cross-model agreement as independent replication without measured error diversity. |

## Implications for ultimateinterview

1. **Evidence must be claim-bound, not merely retrieved.** For every consequential requirement, store the exact source span, source identity/version/date, the claim it supports, and whether it supports, contradicts or is merely related. Retrieval success is not verification.
2. **Separate evidence tools from verification tools.** Search/docs can propose evidence; tests, schema validators, compilers, database queries, deterministic calculators, and authoritative API responses can verify decidable properties. Mark non-executable semantic claims as human- or source-validated, not machine-verified.
3. **Make revision conditional on new evidence.** A reflection/debate round that produces no new source, observation, counterexample, or executable result should not raise confidence or close a gap.
4. **Do not count votes as independent confirmations.** Same model, same prompt, same retrieved context, same model family, or same evaluator are one observation group unless independence is demonstrated. Preserve minority contradictions; do not let consensus erase them.
5. **Use adversarial roles to generate tests, not verdicts.** Debate is valuable for surfacing alternative hypotheses and falsifiers. The verdict should come from an independent human/authority/deterministic check where stakes are high.
6. **Use paired process and outcome gates.** Process obligations catch unsupported leaps and lucky answers; outcome gates check the deliverable's executable/observable contract. A learned verifier or rubric is advisory until calibrated against held-out labeled cases and tested for adversarial false positives.
7. **Require abstention and escalation.** For each decision, define what evidence is sufficient, the maximum tolerated residual risk, and what happens when support is missing or contradictory. “Unknown / needs user decision / needs runtime evidence” must be first-class terminal states.
8. **Calibrate per deployment slice.** Confidence thresholds must be validated for the current model/version, prompt, task class and evidence regime. Invalidate calibration on drift; never map a model's verbal confidence directly to acceptance.
9. **Track three different notions:** factuality (true in the world), faithfulness (entailed by supplied evidence), and requirement validity (accepted by the authorized stakeholder). A result can satisfy one and fail the others.
10. **Stress the verifier.** Before letting a verifier close a gate, test false-positive cases, misleading-but-plausible traces, irrelevant gold-looking context, order effects, verbosity/style perturbations, and increasing candidate search. Cap search when verifier performance ceases to improve.

## OBSERVATIONS

- External, checkable feedback is the most consistent differentiator between helpful correction and unreliable self-revision in the evaluated studies.
- Retrieval reduces some parametric hallucination but adds retrieval errors, distraction, provenance bias, poisoning/freshness risk and correlated common-mode failure.
- Self-consistency is an accuracy technique for certain distributions, not a truth certificate; more samples can strengthen a systematic mistake.
- Debate's strongest empirical advantage is exposing one-sided persuasion and information asymmetry. Homogeneous debate can collapse into confident consensus without truth.
- Verifiers and process reward models improve bounded math selection, but both are proxy models and can be fooled; executable checks provide a qualitatively stronger anchor when available.
- Uncertainty methods identify some errors and enable selective refusal, but calibration is slice-specific and semantic entropy deliberately misses systematic confident errors.

## CLAIMS

- **Supported:** ultimateinterview should accept revisions as higher-confidence only when they add independently checkable evidence or close a deterministic obligation.
- **Supported:** reviewer/sample agreement must not be counted as quorum without measuring independence of model, prompt, context, source and evaluator.
- **Supported:** process review and final-outcome verification are complementary; neither learned step scores nor final-answer checks alone cover lucky answers, flawed traces and verifier gaming.
- **Supported with boundary:** calibrated abstention can control empirical error on exchangeable deployment slices with labeled calibration data; it is not a distribution-free guarantee under drift.
- **Refuted:** “more LLM calls, critics, agents or debate rounds monotonically improve reliability.”
- **Refuted:** “a cited/retrieved passage makes a claim verified.”
- **Unresolved:** whether present debate or verifier gains transfer to open-ended, multi-turn brownfield requirements elicitation; no reviewed paper directly evaluates ultimateinterview's task distribution.

- LEAD: define per-gate abstention calibration datasets — WHY: confidence and semantic-entropy thresholds drift across tasks — ANGLE: held-out real interview decisions labeled by authorized users, with coverage-risk curves and explicit recalibration triggers.
